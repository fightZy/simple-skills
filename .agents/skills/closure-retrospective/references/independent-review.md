# Independent Review

Read only after existing coverage and supported scope have been assessed and an independent perspective could materially change an unresolved, consequential decision.

## Frame the need

Name the unresolved question and how its answer would change the proposal. Broad reach, conflicting evidence, specialized uncertainty, or competing workflow costs can signal a need; none alone requires a reviewer.

Record separately:

- what the evidence supports and what remains uncertain;
- whether review is needed or independently required;
- whether dispatch is already authorized and the capability available.

Missing authorization or capability does not change the observations. An unresolved substantive question still limits the recommendation. Respect the entrypoint's dispatch boundary.

## Prepare and sequence the review

Give each reviewer one bounded question and the minimum relevant packet: source evidence and limitations, exact existing guidance, proposed scope or wording, and the issue to resolve. Ask for evidence, counterexamples, and decision implications without prescribing the desired verdict. Keep the retrospective's evidence boundary intact.

Use multiple reviewers only for distinct questions or expertise. Run independent questions in parallel and dependent questions sequentially, such as evidence correctness before transfer or integration. Do not duplicate a question to collect votes.

## Resolve or leave pending

The main agent checks returned claims against their sources, resolves or preserves material disagreement, and owns the final decision. Apply the evidence rules in [decision-rubric.md](decision-rubric.md): agreement over a shared record cannot stand in for independent empirical coverage.

A proposal is ready only when its evidence, scope, and required review support it. A separately supported narrower proposal may proceed unless an applicable review requirement still governs it.

If review remains necessary, mark that proposal pending and state the question and currently supported scope. Ask for dispatch authorization only if it is missing; report unavailable capability when that is the blocker. Do not seek edit approval for the pending proposal or block unrelated ready proposals.
