# Context Trace

Use this reference when the final state does not provide enough evidence to explain how context or an environment mechanism affected a decision or execution. The purpose is to establish the smallest supported causal account needed for the retrospective, not to produce a complete task history.

## Trace the relevant path

Start from the outcome or decision under review. Follow only earlier decisions or actions that could change the explanation or the proposed change. For each material point, identify:

- the requirements, evidence, assumptions, guidance, tools, and feedback available at that time;
- the decision or action taken from that context;
- the consequence relevant to the review;
- any later evidence or decision that changed its interpretation.

Prefer contemporaneous records over later recollection. Distinguish what the evidence shows from what is inferred. Stop when the context mechanism is established, the remaining history cannot change the conclusion, or the evidence boundary is reached.

## Classify what changed

Do not treat change, iteration, or a poor outcome as proof that guidance failed. Classify the supported explanation as one or more of:

- reasonable adaptation to new requirements, evidence, environment, or constraints;
- missing, stale, conflicting, overly broad, or overly narrow context;
- relevant context that was unavailable or loaded too late;
- an information source, entry point, tool output, or feedback mechanism that was missing, unusable, or poorly integrated;
- guidance that expressed the right intent but lacked an actionable decision condition;
- a decision that was not carried across an ownership or workflow handoff;
- an execution deviation that no evidenced environment context change would improve;
- unresolved because the available evidence cannot distinguish plausible causes.

Existing guidance supports an execution-only conclusion only when it was available at the relevant decision point, sufficiently actionable, consistent with other applicable guidance, and preserved across any handoff that affected the decision.

Available, actionable guidance can still coexist with an evidenced tool or feedback gap; assess that mechanism before dropping a candidate.

## Form a reusable candidate

For each supported context problem, connect:

1. the observed decision or outcome;
2. the context or environment mechanism that influenced it;
3. the future decision or execution outcome that should improve;
4. the narrowest scope in which the improvement is supported.

Drop chronology that does not affect this chain. Do not generalize incidental tools, filenames, actors, values, or domain details unless the proposed change depends on them. If the evidence supports only a task-specific explanation or expected adaptation, retain the finding without proposing a durable change.
